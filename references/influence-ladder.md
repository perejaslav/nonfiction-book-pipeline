# Influence Ladder for Historical Nonfiction

Use this reference when writing chapters about legacy, influence, reception, transmission, or parallels between cultures.

## Level 1 — Direct heritage

Documented continuity or technical inheritance.

Examples:
- script systems;
- administrative practices;
- legal formulas;
- measurement systems;
- calendrical structures;
- named texts copied or translated through known channels.

Language:
- "directly attested"
- "documented continuity"
- "preserved in later copies"
- "transmitted through..."

## Level 2 — Cultural transmission

A motif, institution, or story moves through intermediaries and changes.

Language:
- "the motif was reworked"
- "later tradition transformed..."
- "the story entered a new theological/political frame"
- "a plausible transmission path runs through..."

## Level 3 — Typological parallel

Similar structures appear in similar social, ecological, institutional, or ritual contexts, without proven direct descent.

Language:
- "typological parallel"
- "similar model"
- "broader regional background"
- "comparable solution to a comparable problem"

## Level 4 — Contested analogy

The comparison is suggestive but weakly evidenced. It can be discussed only with explicit caveats.

Language:
- "tempting but uncertain analogy"
- "direct lineage cannot be established"
- "useful comparison, not an origin claim"
- "the similarity should not be overstated"

## Forbidden without strong source

- "X is the origin of Y"
- "Y is just X with new names"
- "this blueprint passed unchanged into..."
- "all later religions inherited this directly"
- "the structure remained the same"

## Safe universal formulas

- "not a direct line of descent, but part of a broader cultural field"
- "one of the earlier layers visible in later traditions"
- "a comparable structure, not proof of borrowing"
- "the motif survived only after deep transformation"
- "the analogy is useful if treated as analogy, not genealogy"
