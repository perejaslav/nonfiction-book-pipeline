# Reconstruction Policy

Use this file to control narrative scenes, dialogue, quotes, and poetic reconstruction in historical nonfiction.

## Direct quotation

Allowed only when:
- the quote comes from a primary source or established translation;
- the source is listed in facts.json;
- the wording is not invented or silently modernized.

Required marker:
- cite source in notes or nearby context.

## Paraphrase of a source

Use when the original exists but wording is condensed.

Preferred formulas:
- "the text says that..."
- "the chronicle describes..."
- "the inscription presents him as..."
- "the myth tells how..."

Do not use quotation marks unless wording is direct.

## Author reconstruction

Use when building a scene from indirect evidence.

Required markers:
- "we can imagine..."
- "a plausible reconstruction would be..."
- "probably..."
- "in a narrative reconstruction..."
- "if we restore the situation from indirect evidence..."

Forbidden:
- invented direct speech in quotation marks;
- invented named witnesses;
- invented letters, papyri, inscriptions, diaries;
- poetic lines presented as ancient hymns unless sourced.

## Dialogue

Default: avoid reconstructed dialogue.

If necessary:
- keep short;
- mark explicitly as reconstruction;
- do not attribute exact words to historical people;
- prefer indirect speech.

## Sensory detail

Allowed when plausible and generic:
- heat, dust, darkness, crowd noise, smell of smoke, texture of clay.

Risky when too specific without source:
- exact room layout;
- named objects in hand;
- precise weather;
- private emotions;
- detailed choreography of rituals.

Use sensory detail to clarify, not to fake certainty.
